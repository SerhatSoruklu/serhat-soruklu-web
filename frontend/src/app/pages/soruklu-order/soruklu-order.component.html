I · The Order

A Small, Voluntary Family Initiative

The Soruklu Order is a small, voluntary family initiative established in 2024 by Serhat Soruklu. It brings together a limited number of consenting members around shared principles, voluntary responsibilities and long-term family continuity.

Membership is selective and voluntary. No person is included merely because of their surname or ancestry. The initiative supports continuity, responsibility, cooperation and careful preservation of authorised records.

Internal responsibilities apply only within the initiative. Its titles do not grant public office, legal authority or control over any person outside it.

The Order is not a government, police force, court, legal authority, military organisation or public institution. It has no authority over people merely sharing the Soruklu surname.

II

Identity boundary

The Order Is Not the Entire Family

The wider Soruklu family consists of many independent households and individuals. The Soruklu Order is a separate voluntary initiative and does not represent every person with the Soruklu surname. Sharing the surname does not imply membership, endorsement, consent or involvement. Participation is based on informed and voluntary consent.

The wider family is not centrally controlled and is not collectively represented by this initiative.

VI · Lawful conduct

Safeguarding, Evidence and Due Process

The Soruklu Order supports the lawful reporting of sexual abuse, violence, exploitation and other serious wrongdoing, and proper investigation by independent authorities. It supports safeguarding vulnerable people, preserving credible evidence, due process, the presumption of innocence and meaningful support for victims and survivors.

These principles apply universally and support individual accountability. They must not be misused to silence victims, excuse harmful conduct or make unsupported collective allegations against an entire family, surname or group of unrelated households.

Reports and investigations belong with independent police, courts, social services, safeguarding authorities, medical services and qualified legal professionals as appropriate. The Order does not replace them or investigate offences itself.
